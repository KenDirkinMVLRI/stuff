01 · The implementation cycle

Four stages. No finish line.

Implementation science is clear: districts don’t “arrive” at innovation — they move through stages over 2–4 years, and then the cycle restarts when the technology, the law, or the evidence changes. Select a stage to see what it demands and what gate you must pass before moving on. AI has changed materially roughly every six months since 2023; a linear rubric can’t survive that. A cycle can.

04 · Implementation drivers

What actually moves the stages

NIRN’s research on why implementations succeed or fail identifies three interdependent driver categories. The 2023 framework implicitly assumed these would take care of themselves. They don’t — they are the work. Every domain above is tagged with its dominant driver.

Competency drivers

Build staff capacity to use AI well — before and during use, never after.

  • Selection: who pilots first, and why
  • Training: role-specific, not one-off webinars
  • Coaching: job-embedded cycles during first use
  • Fidelity: practice rubrics, not seat time

Organization drivers

Build systems that make good practice easy and unsafe practice hard.

  • Data systems that inform decisions (usage, outcomes, equity)
  • Facilitative administration: barriers removed, time protected
  • Systems intervention: vendors, contracts, state & federal alignment

Leadership drivers

Match the leadership style to the problem in front of you.

  • Technical leadership for clear problems (provisioning, DPAs)
  • Adaptive leadership for contested ones (integrity, trust, job fears)
  • Visible modeling: leaders disclose their own AI use
